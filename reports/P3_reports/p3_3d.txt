def node_range(rel, qualname):
    """(first line incl. decorators, last line) of a top-level def/class or Class.method."""
    parts = qualname.split(".")
    body = tree(rel).body

        n = next(x for x in body if isinstance(x, (ast.FunctionDef, ast.AsyncFunctionDef, ast.ClassDef)) and x.name == p)

def enclosing(rel):

                    out[i] = q
                visit(c, q)

                visit(c, prefix)

Builder's answer (c.1), citing ofilt/drift/calib.py:
- (i) `bootstrap_test(frame, spec, rng, target, B=9999, mean_block=5.0)` (150-161) runs D3.1.
  - It fits the DK model once through `bootstrap_t` (139: `fit_t`, which is `inference.build_design` then `inference.fit_dk`, 99-108).
  - It draws B stationary-bootstrap resamples of whole dates with mean block 5 (`bootstrap_t` 143-146; `resample_dates` 111-129, through `sim.stationary_bootstrap_positions`, 121), each refitted at the original DK lag (144).
  - It returns `c_boot_95` and `c_boot_90`, the 0.95 and 0.90 order statistics of |t*| (157-158); the H1 decision `reject`, |t| > c_boot_95 (159); and the interval b ± c_boot_95·se (160).
  - It takes a generator `rng`, not a seed: the caller passes `np.random.default_rng(seed)`.
  - It does not return a TOST decision: TOST at c_boot_90 is a separate call, `tost_declares(b, se, sesoi, c_boot_90)` (docstring, 153). Nor does it return H3's t*: `bootstrap_t` computes `t_star_h3` (146-147), and `bootstrap_test` drops it.
  - Each of these functions calls `blind.require_unblinded(frame)` first (154, 136, 102, 116). On a `confirm_real` frame it therefore runs only when both tags are ancestors. `resample_dates` keeps the input's provenance (116, 128).
- (ii) No single function does all three. The parts are:
  - `fit_t` (99-108): b, se, t = b/se and the lag of the normal-reference DK fit;
  - `rejects(t, c)` (87-89): the two-sided decision |t| > c at a supplied c;
  - `tost_declares(b, se, sesoi, c)` (92-95): TOST at a supplied c.
  - The interval b ± c·se at a supplied c: none. `bootstrap_test` forms it only for its own c_boot_95 (160).
- (iii) None. calib.py has no Clark–West function. The CW statistic and its normal one-sided p-value are in `fctests.clark_west` (listed under c.2). The sealed-run tool applies a calibrated one-sided critical value inline: `tools/p3_sealed_run.py:641`, `v["cw"] > ccw`.
